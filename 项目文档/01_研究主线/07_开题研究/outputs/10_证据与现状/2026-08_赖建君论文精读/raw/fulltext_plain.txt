Abstract
With the development of high multilayer PCB (20 to 40 layers) and the rapid development of flexible printed circuit boards, laser drilling instead of mechanical drilling has become the mainstream processing solutions. With the improvement of chip integration and the used of advanced packaging technology, the blind holes present a trend of miniaturisation. Blind hole drilling accuracy affects the electrical performance of PCBs, and the precision inspection of its processing quality has become a key challenge to ensure the reliability of circuit interconnection. Traditional measurement methods are limited by speed, accuracy and cost, and it is difficult to meet the demand for micro-blind hole morphology detection. To this end, this study designs and implements a high-speed and high-precision surface profile 3D reconstruction system based on spectral confocal sensors, which provides an efficient solution for blind hole quality inspection through the synergistic optimisation of machinery-control-algorithm.
In terms of system construction, a rotary motor-driven ball screw three-axis motion stage is designed in this study. The mechanical design is optimised to reduce the error motion theoretically the real-time control system based on the STM32H7 MCU is constructed using the CANopen protocol, and the transmission delay is reduced by the timertriggered PDO communication. The tracking accuracy and performance of the motion stage is improved by the full-state feedback control method (with the repetition accuracy up to ±0.27 µm), which provides a stable and reliable hardware foundation for the highspeed scanning.
In the design of algorithms, for the noise interference in spectral confocal scanning data, a bidirectional window detection algorithm is proposed to eliminate outliers, adaptive Kalman filtering is used to enhance robustness under dynamic conditions, and the improved BEADS baseline drift correction algorithm combined with parabolic subtraction is used to inhibit the nonlinear signal aberrations. For the lack of positional information in the semiclosed-loop system, a multilevel alignment framework incorporating kinematic constraints is designed to optimize the temporal and spatial consistency of data. In surface reconstruction, the RANSAC algorithm is used to quickly fit the surface primitives of copper foils, and the DBSCAN clustering segmentation and optimisation method is used to achieve the minimum outer circle fitting of the blind hole point cloud, so as to solve

the diameter and depth features. Experimental validation shows that the system exhibits great performance in copper
foil blind hole array detection: the deviation of diameter solving is -3 µm to 9 µm(-3 % to 9 %), and the deviation of depth is ±0.4 µm(±1 %), which meets the stringent requirements of high-density PCBs on blind hole processing quality (typical tolerance ±10 %). Compared with the traditional median filtering, morphological filtering, wavelet decomposition and empirical modal decomposition, the improved BEADS algorithm can be applied to different types of complex surfaces in baseline drift correction, which is more universal. The results show that the system can effectively support the blind hole topography inspection of high multilayer PCBs and flexible circuit boards, providing a reliable technical means for the optimisation of precision electronics manufacturing processes, and has the potential to be extended to scenarios such as the inspection of MEMS devices and wafer-level surface analysis.
